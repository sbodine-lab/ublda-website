# Plain-spoken copy

Write for the reader's actual question. Name the people, work, place, or action before describing its importance. Keep the original meaning, dates, eligibility, links, and distinctions between planned and completed work.

Read the surrounding page before editing. Make each heading describe the section beneath it. Use complete, connected sentences where the idea needs explanation; vary sentence length for a reason. Short sentences are useful, but two parallel fragments stacked as a slogan often sound manufactured. Do not join unrelated ideas with a comma just to make them feel polished. Break a long sentence where the reader needs a pause, or connect clauses with a clear relationship.

During revision, check for:

- Repeated promotional abstractions such as *impact*, *journey*, *purpose*, *empower*, *unlock*, and *transform* where a concrete action would say more.
- Stock LLM-associated words such as *delve*, *tapestry*, *realm*, *intricate*, *underscore*, and *meticulous*. Replace them when ordinary language is more accurate. A word alone does not establish AI authorship.
- Matching sentence openings, rhythmic three-part lists, mirrored clauses, empty contrasts, and a run of headline fragments. Keep parallel structure when it actually helps comparison.
- Comma-heavy sentences that stack benefits, audiences, and aspirations without saying what happened or what will happen.
- Claims that imply experience, partnerships, outcomes, or certainty that the source material does not support.

Read the revision aloud, then inspect it in its actual layout. Keep useful punctuation, technical terms, and brief UI labels; the goal is natural and accurate copy, not mechanical removal of commas or short sentences.
